Most people assume a tattoo brand starts in a tattoo studio. Ink Kulture didn't. It started with a question three friends kept asking at college fests and wedding sangeets: why does wanting a tattoo for one evening mean choosing between a permanent one and a ₹50 sticker that smudges before dinner?

That gap, between forever and fake, is where semi-permanent tattoos live. And it's the gap Ink Kulture was built to fill. This post is about the three people who run the brand day to day, what each of them actually does, and the few beliefs that shape every decision from design to delivery.

The short version

  • Shivang Gupta, CEO leads product, growth and the overall direction of the brand.
  • Sristi Gupta, CFO runs the money: pricing, unit economics, cash flow and the discipline that keeps a fast-moving D2C brand honest.
  • Shivam Mali, COO owns operations: printing, packing, couriers, and the daily grind of getting every order to a doorstep on time.

Ink Kulture is operated by SMG GO Private Limited, based in Navi Mumbai. The three of them are co-directors.

Shivang Gupta, CEO

Shivang is the reason the brand exists. He's the one who looked at the semi-permanent tattoo category in India and saw that nobody was treating it seriously: designs were imported, generic, and clearly made for a different skin tone and a different aesthetic.

His job today splits into three parts. The first is product: which designs get made, what sizes, how the ink develops on brown skin, how long it lasts. Every design in the catalogue, from the small 5.5 cm scripts to the full sleeves, has been through him. The second is growth: the Instagram reels, the creator collaborations, the ads, the website. The third is the part nobody sees, which is deciding what not to do. A small brand dies from doing too many things. Shivang's most useful word is "no".

If you've ever messaged the brand on WhatsApp at midnight and got a real answer, there's a fair chance it was him.

Sristi Gupta, CFO

Sristi keeps the brand alive in the most literal sense. D2C in India runs on cash on delivery, and cash on delivery runs on patience: the courier collects the money, holds it for a week or more, and sends it back minus their charges. Meanwhile ads, printing and packaging have to be paid for today.

Her job is to make that gap survivable. She sets the prices, decides how much of a discount the Buy 1 Get 1 offer can carry, watches the return rate on COD orders like a hawk, and tells the other two when an idea that sounds exciting doesn't actually make money. When Ink Kulture moved to rewarding online payment with a bigger discount, that was a CFO decision, not a marketing one: prepaid orders come back far less often, and that single fact changes the economics of every order.

She's also the one who insists on receipts, reconciliations and the boring spreadsheets that make a brand sellable one day instead of just busy.

Shivam Mali, COO

Shivam runs everything between "order placed" and "order delivered". That sounds simple. It isn't.

A semi-permanent tattoo is a thin printed sheet. It has to be printed cleanly, cut precisely, packed flat so it doesn't crease, and handed to a courier who will actually pick it up today, not "tomorrow, maybe". Then it has to survive an Indian courier network, reach a customer who may or may not be home, and arrive looking like something worth the money.

Shivam built the packing line, chose and keeps switching between courier partners based on who actually delivers in each region, and runs the follow-ups when a delivery fails. He's the reason the brand can promise a dispatch date on the product page and mostly keep it. He's also the person who notices when a courier quietly starts underperforming in one state and moves orders elsewhere before customers notice.

What the three of them agree on

Founders disagree constantly; that's healthy. But a few things are settled at Ink Kulture:

  1. Made for Indian skin. Designs are tested on the skin tones that actually order them. The ink develops darker over 24 to 48 hours, which is why photos on day two look better than day one.
  2. Honest about how long it lasts. Up to two weeks, depending on placement and skin. Not "weeks and weeks". If a customer asks whether it'll survive a beach holiday, they get a straight answer.
  3. Buy 1 Get 1 on every design, always. Not a weekend sale that keeps coming back. The lower-priced design in each pair is free at checkout, no code needed. It's the simplest offer in the category and it stays.
  4. Reply like a person. Order updates, tracking, questions about application: on WhatsApp, in plain language, usually fast.

Where this is going

The next year is about depth, not noise: more designs made for men and for Indian festivals, larger pieces for people who want a sleeve for a wedding week, and faster delivery in the cities where most orders come from. The team is small on purpose. Small teams ship.
